The 0.85→1.00 uptick did not continue — the inversion region shows the same jagged signature as the rest of the curve, with 1.10 an isolated spike that ties the champion on one window while its neighbors sit at 51 and 66. Lessons: (1) orientation B — beta-heavy in drawdowns — beats A on the live window in every pairing, validating the dip-harvest mechanism directionally; (2) the extreme flip is the campaign's cautionary tale: best single number of the whole day (85.8) and the second-worst regime profile (+41) — window-fit in its purest form; (3) no adaptive arm cleared the pre-registered gate, and the strongest challenger (0.30/0.15) is dominated by fixed 0.30 on both axes; (4) the odd couple remains: spy200 (0.15/0.45) wins regimes but loses the recent window — regime-conditioning has real breadth value that this simple SPY-EMA trigger can't capture without giving up recent-window performance. The lever with a mechanism for both: condition on the strategy's own trailing efficacy, not market state — the one untested idea, deliberately excluded from today's scope.

Verdict & recommendation

  1. Keep dose 0.30 as the production default. decided Best measured point on the honest live window (82.7%, 4 seeds), second-best cross-regime sum, already validated twice and wired into the nightly pipeline. Nothing in 0.35–1.00 challenges it; 0.45 is actively destructive.
  2. The real challenger is your adaptive idea, upgraded: next lever spy200 won cross-regime despite its defensive constant (0.45) being the live curve's one hole. The obvious variant — 0.15 above the 200-EMA / 0.30 below (defensive dose = the known-good default instead of the hole) — plausibly dominates both parents. That's a 2-arm test: live window (2 seeds) + 4 deep slices ≈ 45 min of compute, and it must pass the standard gate before touching production.
  3. Stop fine-tuning fixed doses. closed The surface is jagged at ±15pp between adjacent doses; anything inside 0.10–0.30 is defensible and differences within the zone are window-dependent noise. 68 backtests bought this certainty; more grid points would buy none.
  4. Full neutralization is refuted as a config but diagnostic as a finding: the model's factor-aligned component carries real signal in most regimes (removing 100% of it collapses cross-regime performance), yet the majority of the edge is idiosyncratic (dose 1.0 still beat raw on the live window). Partial neutralization is the right family.

Methodology & disclosures

  • Live arms: production 5__NightlyBackTester.py, --data_dir read-only override, BT_SAMPLE_SEED ∈ {42,44} (0.30 reference = 4 seeds from the morning A/B). Deep arms: BT_AS_OF fork, pinned slices, seed 42 (spot-checked bit-identical across seeds).
  • Factor panel for deep arms rebuilt by invoking the production block code per ticker (beta_metrics / volatility_atr / liquidity), float32-cast; parity gate vs the original dose-0.3 arm: max |ΔUpProbability| = 0 across 100 sampled tickers.
  • Disclosure: the first sweep launch had a 10× dose-encoding bug (arm names divided by 1000, not 100). Caught after 8 runs via curve-shape implausibility; those runs are archived as genuine micro-dose data (0.015–0.035) and the entire grid re-ran under the corrected parser. Nothing from the bugged runs is mixed into the tables above.
  • Adaptive dose uses same-day SPY close vs its 200-day EMA (yfinance, fetched through 2026-07-02) — observable state, no lookahead. Per-day dose applied to the same per-day centered projection; quantile-map preserves each day's UpProbability marginal exactly, so no downstream gate/threshold was retuned anywhere in this study.
  • Live prediction set remained RAW throughout (verified at each launch); every backtest ran against copies. The live signals file was backed up and restored after every batch. Deep slice levels carry survivorship bias (survivor-only lake).
